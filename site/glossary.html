Glossary: Every HR Acronym, One Breath Each

Education only — not financial advice. ← Back to start

  • 401(k) — An employer-sponsored retirement account; the money comes out of your paycheck and grows tax-advantaged. Named for a line in the tax code. See 401(k) basics.
  • 403(b) / 457(b) — 401(k) equivalents for nonprofits, schools, and some government jobs. Same core idea.
  • 457(b) special feature — The government version has no early-withdrawal penalty on separation from service. Nice detail, rarely relevant.
  • Beneficiary — The person (or people) who inherits the account if you die. Please actually fill this out. It’s on the plan’s website, takes 2 minutes, and out-of-date beneficiaries have caused real family pain.
  • Catch-up contribution — Extra allowed contribution amount once you’re 50+. Just a rule, not an emergency.
  • Cliff vesting — Match (not your own money) only becomes yours after X years, then all at once. See vesting.
  • Contribution limit — IRS cap on how much you can put in per year. Changes annually. Check IRS.gov for this year’s exact numbers.
  • Direct rollover — Money moves straight from one institution to another, never touching your hands. No tax event. Always request this.
  • Default fund — What your money goes into if you never choose. Usually a TDF. If you’ve never picked anything, you are invested, in this.
  • Expense ratio — Annual fund fee, as a percentage (e.g., 0.08% = 8 cents per $100 invested per year). Under ~0.2% for a TDF is competitive.
  • ERISA — Federal law protecting employee benefit accounts. Part of why 401(k)s are extremely safe from your employer’s own creditors.
  • ESPP — Employee stock purchase plan. Separate topic, not covered here.
  • Fiduciary — Someone legally required to act in your best interest. When you eventually need real advice, ask for a fee-only fiduciary.
  • Glide path — How a target-date fund’s stock/bond mix changes as the target year approaches.
  • Graded vesting — Match ownership arrives in chunks (e.g., 20%/year over 5 years).
  • IRA (Traditional) — A retirement account you open yourself; tax-deferred like a 401(k); smaller limits.
  • IRA (Roth) — A retirement account you open yourself; you pay tax going in and never pay tax on withdrawals or growth. See IRAs.
  • MAGI — Modified Adjusted Gross Income; the income number that drives Roth IRA phase-outs. Tax software computes it; you don’t have to.
  • Match — Employer money added to your account because you contributed. Free money with a formula. See matching.
  • Money market fund / settlement fund — Where cash sits inside a brokerage account before it’s invested. Earning ~nothing in here is the “I forgot to invest my rollover” trap.
  • Mutual fund — A pool of many stocks/bonds you buy shares of, priced once per day. The boring default.
  • Index fund — A mutual fund or ETF that mechanically tracks a whole market (like the S&P 500) instead of picking winners. Usually very low fee.
  • ETF — Like a mutual fund but trades all day like a stock. For retirement accounts, mutual funds and ETFs do effectively the same job.
  • RMD — Required Minimum Distribution: the age (mid-70s) at which traditional accounts must start paying you. Roths: no RMDs.
  • Rebalancing — Periodically restoring your stock/bond mix to your target. TDFs do this automatically forever; you never have to think about it again.
  • Rollover — Moving money from one retirement account to another tax-free. Classic use: old 401(k) → IRA. See rollovers.
  • Roth 401(k) — Same structure as Roth IRA, but higher contributions limits and run through your employer. See Roth vs. Traditional.
  • SIPC — Insurance protecting your securities if a brokerage collapses. (Doesn’t protect you from market losses, which is investing being investing.)
  • Target-date fund (TDF) — One fund holding a self-adjusting mix of stocks and bonds, labeled by your retirement year. The best default for most people. See target-date funds.
  • Vesting — The schedule on which employer match money becomes legally yours. Your own contributions are always vested immediately. See vesting details.

Words people say that aren’t real terms

  • “Settle into it” — Means “the money will show up in a few days.” Fine.
  • “Fully vested, timing nothing” — HR-speak for “you own the match now.” Good.
  • “Your money is parked” — Means literally “it’s sitting in the settlement fund as cash.” Usually means you should invest it.
