The 90-second version

If you remember nothing else from this whole site:

  1. If your job offers a match, contribute at least enough to get the full match. It’s a raise you get for free.
  2. A 401(k) is just a bucket with a tax sticker on it. Pick the sticker (traditional = tax later, Roth = tax now) by contributing to the bucket, not by worrying.
  3. Vesting means the employer match becomes legally yours over time. You can always take your own contributions with you.
  4. A target-date fund with a year near your expected retirement date is a fine starting point for people who want one decision instead of fifty.
  5. Moving to a new job? Do a rollover — direct, or as a hardship-free “no taxes touched” move — instead of cashing out. Cash-outs cost a fortune in taxes and lost growth.

A few honest caveats

  • Contribution limits and rules change almost every year and vary by age and plan. Always check the current numbers at IRS.gov before acting. Any figure in this guide is a snapshot for illustration, not a guarantee.
  • Some investment details (locking a 401(k) early, SEPP rules, mega-backdoor Roth mechanics, mega-geek tax edge cases) are deliberately simplified. When you’re face-to-face with one of them, that’s a “call your plan’s customer service and ask them to walk you through it slowly” moment, not a “wing it” moment.
  • Fees matter more than most people assume. A quick “expense ratio” glance is in the target-date funds doc.
  • This guide never picks specific funds or companies for you. That’s on purpose. You now know enough to do that part yourself, or to ask good questions.
