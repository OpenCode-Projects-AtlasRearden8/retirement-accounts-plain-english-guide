# Retirement Accounts, Explain(ed) Like a Friend Would

*What to actually do with that 401(k) form HR keeps emailing you about.*

**Education only.** This guide explains how retirement accounts work in plain English. It is not financial advice, and it does not recommend any specific product, fund, or company. For decisions about your own money, talk to a fiduciary financial professional you trust.

No tracking, no analytics, no ads. This site and the markdown docs run entirely on your computer.

## Who this is for

You sat through the HR orientation. Someone said words like "vesting schedule," "Roth," "employer match," and "target-date fund." You nodded. You felt like everyone else understood something invisible. You were maybe one raise away from just... not signing anything.

This guide is for you. It assumes zero background, explains each idea once like a friend would, and ends with concrete, low-pressure first steps. If you have a job with benefits (or you're between jobs and have an old 401(k) lying around), this is for you.

## The 90-second version

If you remember nothing else from this whole site:

1. **If your job offers a match, contribute at least enough to get the full match.** It's a raise you get for free.
2. **A 401(k) is just a bucket** with a tax sticker on it. Pick the sticker (traditional = tax later, Roth = tax now) by contributing to the bucket, not by worrying.
3. **Vesting** means the employer match becomes *legally yours* over time. You can always take your own contributions with you.
4. **A target-date fund with a year near your expected retirement date** is a fine starting point for people who want one decision instead of fifty.
5. **Moving to a new job?** Do a **rollover** — direct, or as a hardship-free "no taxes touched" move — instead of cashing out. Cash-outs cost a fortune in taxes and lost growth.

## A few honest caveats

- Contribution limits and rules change almost every year and vary by age and plan. **Always check the current numbers at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans)** before acting. Any figure in this guide is a snapshot for illustration, not a guarantee.
- Some investment details (locking a 401(k) early, SEPP rules, mega-backdoor Roth mechanics, mega-geek tax edge cases) are deliberately simplified. When you're face-to-face with one of them, that's a "call your plan's customer service and ask them to walk you through it slowly" moment, not a "wing it" moment.
- Fees matter more than most people assume. A quick "expense ratio" glance is in the [target-date funds doc](target-date-funds.md).
- This guide never picks specific funds or companies for you. That's on purpose. You now know enough to do that part yourself, or to ask good questions.
